Positive Psychology

School·1998·积极心理学

Psychology should study not only illness but also how people live well: well-being, strengths, meaning, engagement and good relationships.

Historical influence ●●●○○·Evidence: Mixed findings·Public familiarity ●●●●○

Thread
Humanism and Meaning
Landmark
Seligman, while president of the American Psychological Association, advocates studying human strengths and well-being
What the evidence says
Some simple interventions (such as gratitude exercises) have small but stable effects. Early headline findings such as the 'positivity ratio' were retracted, and overall research quality varies.
Key figure
Martin Seligman

Core theories

  • Hedonic Adaptation 1971Whether someone wins a lottery or is injured, their emotional reaction to the change usually gradually returns to their earlier level.
  • Broaden-and-Build Theory 1998Positive emotions widen a person's attention and thinking, helping them build long-term resources such as skills, friendships, and resilience.
  • Growth Mindset 2006People who believe ability can be changed through effort are more willing to take on challenges. People who believe ability is fixed tend to avoid failure.
  • Grit 2007Perseverance and passion for long-term goals are thought to predict achievement better than talent does.
  • PERMA Model of Well-Being 2011Well-being has five parts: positive emotion, engagement, relationships, meaning, and accomplishment.

Key concepts

  • Hedonic vs. Eudaimonic Well-BeingWell-being comes in two kinds: feeling good and living meaningfully.

Relations to other schools

  • ← Develops FlowCsikszentmihalyi is a shared advocate of positive psychology.
  • ← Develops Self-Determination TheoryAutonomy, competence and relatedness become central to well-being research.
  • ← Develops LogotherapyMeaning becomes one dimension of the model of happiness.
  • ← Develops Learned HelplessnessSeligman moved from studying helplessness to studying optimism and well-being.
  • ← Develops Hierarchy of Needs and Self-ActualizationInherits the focus on growth and potential.
  • → Critiques Hierarchy of Needs and Self-ActualizationCriticizes humanistic psychology for lacking empirical method and calls for measurement and experiment.
  • ← Critiques Second Wave Positive PsychologyFocuses only on the positive, ignoring the place of suffering in a good life.
