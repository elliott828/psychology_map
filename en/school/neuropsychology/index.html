Neuropsychology

School·1957·神经心理学

By studying what brain-damaged patients lose, it infers which brain regions normal mental functions depend on, and it also develops clinical tools for assessing brain injury.

Historical influence ●●●●○·Evidence: Strong evidence·Public familiarity ●●○○○

Thread
Brain and Biology
Landmark
Scoville and Milner report patient H.M.: after removal of the medial temporal lobes, he could not form new long-term memories
What the evidence says
Double dissociation and case and group study methods are mature, and they form one of the evidence bases of cognitive neuroscience.
Key figure
William Beecher Scoville

Core theories

  • Dynamic Localization of Higher Functions 1962Complex mental activity depends on several brain areas working together. Damage to one area can affect many functions. Systematic case analysis is needed to find which link has been damaged.
  • Multiple Memory Systems 1980There is more than one kind of memory. Facts and events that can be stated depend on the hippocampus, while skill memories such as riding a bike or typing depend on other brain areas. This is why people with amnesia can still learn new skills.
