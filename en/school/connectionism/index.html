Connectionism

School·1986·联结主义

Uses networks of many simple connected units to model the mind. Knowledge is not stored in one place but distributed across the strengths of connections, adjusted through learning.

Historical influence ●●●○○·Evidence: Supported, with limits·Public familiarity ●●○○○

Thread
Mind and Cognition
Landmark
Rumelhart and McClelland's Parallel Distributed Processing
What the evidence says
Neural network models can reproduce many learning and perception phenomena, but whether they reflect the brain's real mechanisms is still debated.
Key figure
David Rumelhart

Core theories

  • The Past-Tense Debate 1986When children learn past tenses such as 'walked' and 'went', are they memorizing each word, or learning a rule? Connectionist and rule-based accounts each offer an explanation.
