Cognitive Dissonance

School·1957·认知失调

When a person's ideas and behavior contradict each other, they feel uncomfortable and tend to change their ideas to ease the discomfort, rather than changing their behavior.

Historical influence ●●●○○·Evidence: Supported, with limits·Public familiarity ●●●●○

What the evidence says
The core phenomenon of self-justification is stable, but whether dissonance is the only source of motivation, and whether it requires a threat to the self, remain debated.
Key figure
Leon Festinger

Core theories

  • Cognitive Dissonance Theory 1957When two ideas conflict, the mismatch is uncomfortable. To reduce this discomfort, people are more likely to adjust their ideas than to change their behavior.
  • Forced Compliance Study 1959Participants did a boring task and told the next person it was interesting. Those paid one dollar rated the task as more enjoyable than those paid twenty dollars, because a dollar gave them too little outside justification.
  • Self-Perception Theory 1967People do not always know what they think. Like observers, they infer from their own behavior that they probably like something.
