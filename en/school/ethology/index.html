Ethology

School·1935·动物行为学

Studies animal behavior in natural settings, treating instincts and imprinting as innate tendencies shaped by evolution, and asks what they are for and where they come from.

Historical influence ●●●○○·Evidence: Supported, with limits·Public familiarity ●●○○○

Thread
Brain and Biology
Landmark
Lorenz publishes his research on imprinting in young birds
What the evidence says
Evidence for imprinting and instinctive behavior patterns in birds and other animals is strong; extending it to humans requires caution.
Key figure
Konrad Lorenz

Core theories

  • Imprinting and Sensitive Periods 1935A newly hatched gosling takes the first moving object it sees as its 'mother' and follows it for a long time. This is seen as an example of early experience shaping behavior in a way that cannot be undone.
  • Tinbergen's Four Questions 1963Explaining a behavior means answering four different questions: what mechanism produces it, how it develops, what adaptive function it serves, and what its evolutionary history is.
  • Multi-level Biology of Behavior 2017The same behavior can be explained layer by layer across time scales: the nerve firing of the previous second, hormones of a few hours earlier, childhood experience, prenatal development, and culture and genetic evolution.

Key people

  • Konrad Lorenz 1903–1989
  • Niko Tinbergen 1907–1988

Key concepts

  • Critical / Sensitive PeriodSome abilities form most easily during specific stages of development.
  • ImprintingYoung animals quickly identify and follow the first object they encounter.
