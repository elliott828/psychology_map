Common Factors

School·1961·共同因素

Different therapies are all effective because they share: a good relationship, hope and expectation, a credible explanation, and opportunities for action.

Historical influence ●●●○○·Evidence: Strong evidence·Public familiarity ●○○○○

Thread
Clinical and Mental Health
Landmark
Frank's Persuasion and Healing: the factors shared by all therapies explain most of the outcome
What the evidence says
Hundreds of studies support the relationship between the therapeutic alliance and outcome. 'All therapies are equally effective' is considered too absolute.
Key figure
Jerome Frank

Core theories

  • Dodo Bird Verdict 1975In 'Alice's Adventures in Wonderland', the Dodo says 'everyone has won and all must have prizes'. The average effects of different psychotherapies are about the same.
  • Working Alliance 1979Whether therapist and client agree on goals and methods, and whether they trust each other, is one of the most stable predictors of therapy outcome.
  • Contextual Model of Psychotherapy 2001Therapy works through three routes: a real relationship, expectations of change, and a credible plan of action offered by the therapist. Specific techniques are only one part of this.
