Neuron Doctrine

School·1891·神经元学说

The nervous system is not a continuous net, but is made of separate nerve cells (neurons) that pass signals to one another at points of contact.

Historical influence ●●●○○·Evidence: Strong evidence·Public familiarity ●○○○○

Thread
Brain and Biology
Landmark
Waldeyer coins the term 'neuron,' summarizing the observations of Cajal and others
What the evidence says
The nervous system is made of separate nerve cells that signal across synapses; this is now a basic fact of neuroscience.
Key figure
Santiago Ramon y Cajal

Core theories

  • Neuron Theory 1888The nervous system is not one continuous network. It is made of countless separate nerve cells that pass signals through contact with one another. This doctrine replaced the 'reticular theory' and laid the groundwork for today's talk of neural circuits.
