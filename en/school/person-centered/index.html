Person-Centered Therapy

School·1951·来访者中心疗法

The key to therapy lies not in technique but in whether the therapist offers empathy, genuineness and unconditional acceptance. In such a relationship, people naturally change in the direction of growth.

Historical influence ●●●●○·Evidence: Supported, with limits·Public familiarity ●●●○○

Landmark
Rogers's Client-Centered Therapy
What the evidence says
Empathy, respect and genuineness are indeed related to therapy outcomes, but they are more like conditions that are necessary but not sufficient.
Key figure
Carl Rogers

Core theories

  • Necessary and Sufficient Conditions 1957When the therapist provides empathy, congruence (genuineness), and unconditional positive regard, the client changes in constructive ways.
