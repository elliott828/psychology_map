Psychopharmacology

School·1952·精神药理学

Uses drugs to change neurotransmitter systems and relieve psychiatric symptoms. It allowed many patients to leave long-term hospital care, but it also left behind the oversimplified claim that mental illness is a chemical imbalance in the brain.

Historical influence ●●●●○·Evidence: Supported, with limits·Public familiarity ●●●○○

Thread
Brain and Biology
Landmark
Chlorpromazine is first used in psychiatry; in France, Delay and Deniker report its effect on psychotic symptoms
What the evidence says
Several drug classes reliably relieve symptoms, but 'chemical imbalance' explanations of cause lack evidence.
Key figure
Jean Delay

Core theories

  • Dopamine Hypothesis of Schizophrenia 1963The hallucinations and delusions of schizophrenia are linked to a dysregulated dopamine signal in the brain. All effective antipsychotic drugs block dopamine receptors.
  • Monoamine Hypothesis of Depression 1965Depression comes from a shortage of certain brain neurotransmitters, namely norepinephrine and serotonin. The idea inspired antidepressant drugs, but it is often misreported as a simple 'lack of happy chemicals'.
