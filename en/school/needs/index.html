Hierarchy of Needs and Self-Actualization

School·1943·需求层次与自我实现

Human motivation moves from survival, safety, belonging and esteem up to self-actualization. Higher growth needs become the main drive only after more basic needs are met.

Historical influence ●●●○○·Evidence: Mixed findings·Public familiarity ●●●●●

Thread
Humanism and Meaning
Landmark
Maslow publishes A Theory of Human Motivation
What the evidence says
'Need satisfaction is related to happiness' has support. The strict hierarchical order has not been confirmed, and the ranking of needs is not fixed across cultures.
Key figure
Abraham Maslow

Core theories

  • Hierarchy of Needs 1943Physiological, safety, love and belonging, esteem, and self-actualization. These are the need categories Maslow arranged. He did not think they were strictly 'unlocked' one level at a time.
  • Self-Actualization Theory 1954A small number of people fully develop their potential. They accept reality, are creative, care for others, and often have 'peak experiences'.

Key people

  • Abraham Maslow 1908–1970

Key concepts

  • Peak ExperienceA brief, intense moment of meaningful experience.

Relations to other schools

  • → Bridges Self-Determination TheoryValues growth needs but replaces the fixed hierarchy with three basic needs.

  • ← Bridges Analytical PsychologyThe ideas of individuation and self-actualization are related, though with no direct teacher-student line.
  • → Develops Positive PsychologyInherits the focus on growth and potential.
  • ← Critiques Positive PsychologyCriticizes humanistic psychology for lacking empirical method and calls for measurement and experiment.
