Affective Neuroscience

School·1994·情感神经科学

Studies how emotion, threat responses, bodily states and decision-making work in the brain. One of its core findings is that emotion is not the enemy of reason.

Historical influence ●●●○○·Evidence: Mixed findings·Public familiarity ●●●○○

Thread
Brain and Biology
Landmark
Damasio's Descartes' Error argues that emotion and bodily signals take part in rational decision-making
What the evidence says
Evidence for brain regions linked to emotion and decision-making is reliable; the claims that 'the amygdala is the fear center' and that fixed basic-emotion circuits exist have been revised.
Key figure
Antonio Damasio

Core theories

  • Basic Emotions 1971Six or seven emotions, such as happiness, anger, and fear, have facial expressions that are common across cultures. This view is widely used in popular science and continues to be questioned by researchers.
  • Somatic Marker Hypothesis 1994When making decisions, emotional reactions in the body tag options as 'good' or 'bad'. Without these tags, even intelligent people make poor decisions.
  • Amygdala Fear Circuit 1996A threatening stimulus can bypass the cortex and trigger the amygdala directly through the thalamus. This means a person can feel afraid before they have fully thought it through.
  • Primary Emotion Systems 1998Mammalian brains contain innate emotional circuits, such as 'seeking', 'fear', and 'care'. They come before thought and drive behavior.
  • Theory of Constructed Emotion 2017Emotions are not a ready-made 'anger circuit' in the brain that gets triggered. Instead, the brain uses body signals and past experience to sort a mass of feeling into 'anger' or 'anxiety'.
