Behavior Therapy

School·1958·行为治疗

Sees symptoms as learned responses and uses learning principles to change them: gradually facing the feared object in a safe setting until the fear fades.

Historical influence ●●●●○·Evidence: Strong evidence·Public familiarity ●●●○○

Thread
Learning and Behavior
Landmark
Wolpe publishes Psychotherapy by Reciprocal Inhibition, which describes systematic desensitization for phobias
What the evidence says
Behavioral techniques such as exposure have reliable efficacy for phobias, OCD and PTSD, and are a cornerstone of evidence-based treatment.
Key figure
Joseph Wolpe

Core theories

  • Systematic Desensitization 1958The person first learns to relax, then imagines or faces the feared situation step by step, from mild to severe. Anxiety is gradually reduced.

Key people

  • Joseph Wolpe 1915–1997
  • Hans Eysenck 1916–1997

Key concepts

  • CounterconditioningRepeatedly pairing a fear-provoking stimulus with a response that is opposite to fear, such as relaxation.

Relations to other schools

  • ← Develops Classical ConditioningWolpe's desensitization builds directly on conditioning and extinction.
  • → Bridges Applied Behavior AnalysisBehavior therapy and applied behavior analysis share learning principles but developed separately.
  • → Merges Cognitive-Behavioral TherapyTechniques such as exposure and behavioral activation are folded into the integrated model.
  • ← Reacts Classical PsychoanalysisEysenck and Wolpe criticized psychoanalysis for lacking evidence of efficacy and advocated treatment based on learning principles.
