Third-Wave Therapies

School·1991·第三浪潮

Rather than mainly changing the content of thoughts, these therapies change a person's relationship to thoughts and emotions: acceptance, mindfulness, defusion, and acting toward valued goals.

Historical influence ●●●○○·Evidence: Supported, with limits·Public familiarity ●●○○○

Thread
Clinical and Mental Health
Landmark
Linehan publishes the first randomized controlled trial of dialectical behavior therapy (ACT followed in 1999 and MBCT in 2002)
What the evidence says
There is support for borderline personality disorder and recurrent depression. On many problems its effects are similar to traditional CBT, and some studies have substantial bias.
Key figure
Marsha Linehan

Core theories

  • Dialectical Behavior Therapy 1993Holds a dialectic between accepting the current situation and changing it. Clients are taught four skill sets: mindfulness, distress tolerance, emotion regulation, and interpersonal effectiveness.
  • Acceptance and Commitment Therapy 1999Six processes: acceptance, cognitive defusion, being present, self as context, values, and committed action. The goal is to keep doing valued things even when pain is present.
  • Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy 2002Teaches people who have been depressed several times to see thoughts as 'just thoughts' rather than facts when their mood begins to sink, so they avoid slipping back into relapse.
  • Compassion-Focused Therapy 2005Emotion regulation is divided into three systems: threat, drive, and soothing. Self-compassion is cultivated to soothe people who have long lived in a threat state.

Key concepts

  • Psychological FlexibilityBeing able to act on your values even when it is uncomfortable.
  • Radical AcceptanceFully acknowledging that reality is what it is, without approving of it.
