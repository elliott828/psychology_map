Cognitive Revolution

School·1956·认知革命

Using the computer as an analogy, it sees the mind as a system that takes in, stores and transforms information, making 'internal processes' a legitimate object of scientific study again.

Historical influence ●●●●●·Evidence: Strong evidence·Public familiarity ●●○○○

Thread
Mind and Cognition
Landmark
An MIT symposium on information theory; Miller publishes 'The Magical Number Seven, Plus or Minus Two'
What the evidence says
Using the language of information processing to study attention, memory and language has produced many replicable findings.
Key figure
George A. Miller

Core theories

  • The Magical Number Seven, Plus or Minus Two 1956The number of units a person can hold in short-term memory is limited, about seven. But this can be expanded through chunking, which combines several items into one.
  • Universal Grammar 1957Humans are born with innate knowledge about the structure of language, which is why children learn grammar quickly from limited input.
  • Information-Processing View 1958People are treated as information channels with limited capacity, and information must pass through stages such as selection, encoding, and storage. This vocabulary became the common model in cognitive research.
  • Chomsky's Review of Verbal Behavior 1959Language creativity, the ability to produce sentences never heard before, cannot be explained by stimulus and reinforcement. It requires an internal system of rules.
