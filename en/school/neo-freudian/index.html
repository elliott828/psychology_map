Neo-Freudian School

School·1937·新弗洛伊德学派

Keeps the view of unconscious conflict but holds that culture and interpersonal relationships matter more than sexual instincts, as in Horney's 'basic anxiety.'

Historical influence ●●○○○·Evidence: Little research·Public familiarity ●●○○○

Thread
Unconscious and Depth
Landmark
Horney's The Neurotic Personality of Our Time
What the evidence says
The direction of emphasizing cultural and interpersonal factors was later widely adopted, but specific theories have seen little empirical testing.
Key figure
Karen Horney

Core theories

  • Basic Anxiety and Neurotic Trends 1937Children raised in environments that lack security develop 'basic anxiety'. They then adopt one of three strategies: moving toward others, moving against others, or moving away from others.
  • Interpersonal Theory of Psychiatry 1953Personality is not something inside a person alone. It is a pattern of recurring interpersonal situations. Anxiety comes from the experience of not being approved of by significant others.

Key people

  • Karen Horney 1885–1952
  • Harry Stack Sullivan 1892–1949
  • Erich Fromm 1900–1980

Key concepts

  • Idealized SelfA perfect image a person builds to cope with inner insecurity.

Relations to other schools
