Big Five Personality

School·1985·大五人格

Most personality differences can be described with five dimensions: openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness and neuroticism. Each dimension is continuous, and people fall at different points along it.

Historical influence ●●●●○·Evidence: Strong evidence·Public familiarity ●●●●○

Thread
Individual Differences and Measurement
Landmark
Costa and McCrae publish the NEO personality inventory, popularizing the five-factor model
What the evidence says
The five dimensions are broadly replicable across many languages and cultures, and they stably predict academic performance, career and health. It is a descriptive model and does not explain causes.
Key figure
Paul Costa

Core theories

  • Five-Factor Model 1992Openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness, and neuroticism. Each of the five dimensions can be divided into facets, and every person has a position on each dimension.
  • Personality Continuity and Maturity 2000A person's position in the population on personality traits is quite stable from middle age onward. At the same time, most people become more conscientious, more settled, and less anxious as they age.
