Stress Physiology

School·1936·压力生理

When the body faces a threat, it triggers a hormonal response. If the response stays on for a long time, it damages the immune system, the cardiovascular system and the brain.

Historical influence ●●●○○·Evidence: Supported, with limits·Public familiarity ●●●○○

Thread
Brain and Biology
Landmark
Selye proposes the 'general adaptation syndrome'
What the evidence says
Evidence that the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis and chronic stress harm the body is solid; Selye's three-stage model itself has since been refined.
Key figure
Hans Selye

Core theories

  • Fight-or-Flight Response 1915When faced with a threat, the sympathetic nervous system and adrenaline speed up the heart and send blood to the muscles, preparing the body to fight or run.
  • General Adaptation Syndrome 1936Whatever the stimulus, the body passes through three stages: alarm, resistance, and exhaustion. Staying in the resistance stage for a long time wears the body down.
  • Transactional Model of Stress and Coping 1984Stress depends not only on the event itself, but on how you appraise it (as a threat or a challenge) and how many resources you believe you have.
  • Glucocorticoid Cascade Hypothesis 1986Stress hormones that stay high for a long time damage neurons in the hippocampus. The hippocampus helps apply the brakes on the stress response, which creates a cycle in which stress makes it harder to stop stress.
  • Allostatic Load 1993The body adjusts constantly to cope with stress. The cost of repeated adjustment over time builds up as 'load', showing up as an overall worsening of blood pressure, blood sugar, inflammation, and other markers.
