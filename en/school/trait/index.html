Trait Theory

School·1937·特质论

Sees personality as a set of relatively stable tendencies: some people are always extraverted, anxious or conscientious, and these can be measured and compared.

Historical influence ●●●●○·Evidence: Strong evidence·Public familiarity ●●●○○

Thread
Individual Differences and Measurement
Landmark
Allport's Personality: A Psychological Interpretation argues that personality is a set of stable, individual tendencies
What the evidence says
Personality traits show stability over time and have predictive power, but they predict behavior in specific situations less well.
Key figure
Gordon Allport

Core theories

  • Lexical Hypothesis 1936In human language, every important individual difference gradually acquires a word to describe it. So the basic dimensions of personality can be found by analyzing the dictionary.
  • Myers-Briggs Type Indicator (MBTI) 1962Turns Jung's typology into a self-report questionnaire. People are placed at one pole on each of four pairs (extraversion-introversion, sensing-intuition, thinking-feeling, judging-perceiving), which combine into 16 types.

Key people

  • Katharine Cook Briggs 1875–1968
  • Gordon Allport 1897–1967
  • Isabel Briggs Myers 1897–1980

Key concepts

  • Barnum EffectPeople are willing to believe vague descriptions that seem “spot on” for them.
  • Trait vs. StateStable tendencies must be distinguished from momentary states.

Relations to other schools

  • → Develops Factor-Analytic Personality ModelsFactor analysis organizes trait words into a few dimensions.
  • → Develops Big Five PersonalityThe lexical hypothesis and factor analysis converge on five basic dimensions.
  • ← Critiques Person-Situation DebateCross-situational consistency of behavior is lower than trait theory assumes.
