Test Theory (Reliability and Validity)

School·1955·测验理论（信度与效度）

Whether a test is usable depends on answering two questions: is it consistent (reliability), and does it measure what it is meant to measure (validity)?

Historical influence ●●●●○·Evidence: Strong evidence·Public familiarity ●○○○○

Thread
Individual Differences and Measurement
Landmark
Cronbach and Meehl propose construct validity
What the evidence says
Reliability, validity and item response theory form the consensus of modern measurement; the specific methods used are still being improved.
Key figure
Lee Cronbach

Core theories

  • Reliability and Validity 1951Reliability means getting similar results on repeated measurement. Validity means the test really measures what it is meant to measure. High reliability does not guarantee high validity.
  • Construct Validity 1955For invisible constructs such as 'intelligence' or 'depression', a whole body of evidence is needed to show a test really measures them. It should correlate with related measures, not correlate with unrelated ones, and predict what it should predict.
  • Item Response Theory 1960Each test item has its own difficulty, and each person has their own ability. The probability of answering correctly is determined by the difference between the two, so the same ability can be measured with items of different difficulty.
