Social Identity Theory

School·1979·社会认同理论

People treat the groups they belong to as part of the 'self,' so they favor their own group and draw self-esteem from the group's status.

Historical influence ●●●●○·Evidence: Strong evidence·Public familiarity ●●●○○

Thread
Society and Culture
Landmark
Tajfel and Turner publish 'An Integrative Theory of Intergroup Conflict'
What the evidence says
The minimal group paradigm and in-group favoritism have been replicated many times. Its explanatory power for real-world conflict is limited and needs supplementing with factors such as group interests.
Key figure
Henri Tajfel

Core theories

  • Minimal Group Paradigm 1971Simply being randomly assigned to a 'red group' or a 'blue group' leads people to give more resources to members of their own group.
  • Social Identity Theory (Core) 1979Once a person places themselves in a group, they protect their self-esteem by favoring their own group. This produces preference for the in-group and prejudice against outsiders.
