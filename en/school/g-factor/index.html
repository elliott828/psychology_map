General Intelligence and Factor Analysis

School·1904·g 因素与因子分析

Scores on different intelligence tests are positively correlated, which suggests a common 'general ability' may lie behind them. Factor analysis is the statistical method used to find it.

Historical influence ●●●●○·Evidence: Strong evidence·Public familiarity ●●○○○

Thread
Individual Differences and Measurement
Landmark
Spearman publishes a paper using factor analysis to propose a 'general intelligence,' g
What the evidence says
The general positive correlation among cognitive tests (positive manifold) is one of the most solid findings in psychology; what g actually represents remains debated.
Key figure
Charles Spearman

Core theories

  • Two-Factor Theory of Intelligence 1904Each test score is determined by two parts: a general ability shared by all tests, called g, and a specific ability unique to that test, called s.
  • Fluid and Crystallized Intelligence 1963Fluid intelligence is the ability to handle new problems, and it declines gradually after middle age. Crystallized intelligence is accumulated knowledge and skill, which can be maintained into old age.
  • Cattell-Horn-Carroll Model 1993Abilities can be arranged in three layers. At the top is general intelligence, g. In the middle are about ten broad abilities. At the bottom are many specific skills.
