Gestalt Psychology

School·1912·格式塔心理学

What we see is a whole, not a sum of parts: some dots automatically form a figure based on proximity and similarity, and the moment of solving a problem can be a reorganization of the whole.

Historical influence ●●●●○·Evidence: Supported, with limits·Public familiarity ●●●○○

Thread
Mind and Cognition
Landmark
Wertheimer publishes his paper on apparent motion: two light points flashed in succession are seen as one moving point
What the evidence says
The principles of perceptual organization are firmly established as descriptions; the physiological assumption of 'brain fields' has been abandoned.
Key figure
Max Wertheimer

Core theories

  • Insight in Problem Solving 1917Solving a problem is often not a matter of step-by-step trial and error. It can come suddenly as an 'aha' after the problem is reinterpreted.
  • Gestalt Principles of Perception 1923Perception automatically groups elements that are close together, look similar, or form a closed shape. The whole is more than the sum of its parts.

Key people

Key concepts

  • Figure-GroundPerception always treats some parts as the “figure” and the rest as the “ground.”
