Prejudice Research

School·1954·偏见研究

Prejudice is not a problem of a few bad people but the result of ordinary people's categorization, group membership and competition. Contact under the right conditions can reduce it.

Historical influence ●●●○○·Evidence: Strong evidence·Public familiarity ●●●○○

Thread
Society and Culture
Landmark
Allport's The Nature of Prejudice: a systematic analysis of how prejudice forms and how it can be reduced
What the evidence says
The contact hypothesis has good support in large meta-analyses. The predictive power of implicit bias tests for individual behavior is weaker.
Key figure
Gordon Allport

Core theories

  • The Authoritarian Personality 1950People who are submissive to authority and hostile to outsiders were thought to have experienced harsh discipline in childhood. The explanation sparked a great deal of research and drew many criticisms.
  • Contact Hypothesis 1954When people from different groups meet under conditions of equal status, shared goals, cooperation, and institutional support, prejudice decreases.
  • Stereotype Threat 1995When people worry that their performance will confirm negative views about their group, they actually perform worse.
  • IAT and Implicit Bias 1998The Implicit Association Test measures people's automatic associations between social groups and good or bad words, using differences in response speed. These results are used to estimate 'implicit bias'.
