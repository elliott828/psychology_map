Schema and Reconstructive Memory

School·1932·图式与记忆重构

Memory is not a matter of playing back a recording of the past. Instead, existing knowledge frameworks (schemas) rebuild it at recall, which is why memories become distorted.

Historical influence ●●●○○·Evidence: Supported, with limits·Public familiarity ●●○○○

Thread
Mind and Cognition
Landmark
Bartlett's Remembering: when people recall an unfamiliar story, they rewrite it according to their own cultural schemas
What the evidence says
The broad point that memory is shaped by prior knowledge is well established; his original experiments were messier than textbooks describe.
Key figure
Frederic Bartlett

Core theories

  • Reconstructive Memory 1932When recalling, people fill in fragmentary pieces with existing knowledge. They often also reshape the memory, naturally, to fit their expectations.
  • Misinformation Effect 1974Suggestive information received after an event quietly changes a person's memory of the original event, and people can feel very sure of the altered memory.
  • Seven Sins of Memory 1999Seven kinds of memory error: transience, absent-mindedness, blocking, misattribution, suggestibility, bias, and persistence. Many of them are side effects of normal memory.
