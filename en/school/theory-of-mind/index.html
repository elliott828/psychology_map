Theory of Mind

School·1983·心理理论

How people come to understand that others' minds differ from their own: knowing that others have their own beliefs and desires, and act on them.

Historical influence ●●●○○·Evidence: Strong evidence·Public familiarity ●●○○○

Thread
Lifespan Development
Landmark
Wimmer and Perner design the 'false belief task' to test whether children understand that others can hold mistaken ideas
What the evidence says
There is much cross-cultural evidence for a turning point at ages three to five, when children understand false beliefs. Earlier implicit understanding is still debated.
Key figure
Heinz Wimmer

Core theories

  • False-Belief Task 1983A child sees a toy moved, then is asked where the person who left will look for it. Most children under four say 'the new place', showing they do not yet understand that others can hold mistaken ideas.
  • Mindblindness Hypothesis 1985Autistic children find it hard to understand other people's thoughts and feelings because they lack, or acquire late, a 'theory of mind'.
