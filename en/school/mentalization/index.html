Mentalization

School·1991·心智化

The ability to understand one's own and others' behavior in terms of thoughts, feelings and wishes. This ability grows out of early experiences of being understood; when it is lacking, emotional dysregulation and relationship difficulties tend to follow.

Historical influence ●●○○○·Evidence: Supported, with limits·Public familiarity ●○○○○

Thread
Unconscious and Depth
Landmark
Fonagy and colleagues' papers on 'reflective functioning': whether caregivers can treat an infant as a being with a mind
What the evidence says
Mentalization-based treatment (MBT) has support from several controlled trials for borderline personality disorder. The concept itself still has measurement debates.
Key figure
Peter Fonagy

Core theories

  • Reflective Function 1991Whether a mother can understand her infant's behavior as coming from a person with thoughts and feelings shapes whether the child can later understand themselves and others.
  • Mentalization-Based Treatment 1999The core of treatment is to help the patient pause when emotions run out of control, and try to think about their own and others' thoughts and feelings.
