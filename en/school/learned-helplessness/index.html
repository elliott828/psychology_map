Learned Helplessness

School·1967·习得性无助

After long exposure to negative events that cannot be controlled, people or animals stop trying, even when the environment later becomes controllable. It was once used to explain depression.

Historical influence ●●●○○·Evidence: Supported, with limits·Public familiarity ●●●○○

Thread
Learning and Behavior
Landmark
Seligman and Maier's experiment: dogs that could not escape electric shocks later stopped trying to escape
What the evidence says
The phenomenon is reliable in animal and some human experiments, but later neuroscience suggests that passivity is the default response, and what has to be learned is 'controllability.'
Key figure
Martin Seligman

Core theories

  • Explanatory Style (Reformulation) 1978When something bad happens, people who habitually see the cause as 'my fault, lasting, and affecting everything' are more likely to fall into helplessness and depression.
  • Learned Helplessness Revised 2016Helplessness is not something 'learned'. It is the default reaction to stress. What actually has to be learned is 'I can control this'.

Key people

  • Martin Seligman 1942–

Key concepts

  • ControllabilityThe experience that “nothing I do makes a difference” is key to learned helplessness.
