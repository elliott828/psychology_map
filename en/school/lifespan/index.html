Life-Span Developmental Psychology

School·1980·毕生发展心理学

Development does not stop at childhood or adolescence; it continues into old age, and includes both growth and decline. The interaction between the individual and the historical era cannot be ignored.

Historical influence ●●●○○·Evidence: Supported, with limits·Public familiarity ●●○○○

Thread
Lifespan Development
Landmark
Baltes, Reese and Lipsitt publish 'Life-Span Developmental Psychology' in the Annual Review of Psychology
What the evidence says
The view that development runs through the whole life, with both gains and losses, is supported by research on aging and cognitive aging. The strength of evidence for individual models varies.
Key figure
Paul Baltes

Core theories

  • Life-Span Propositions 1987Development is lifelong, multidirectional, and involves both gains and losses. It is shaped by three kinds of influence: age, history, and individual life events.
  • Selection, Optimization and Compensation 1990As resources decline, older adults keep functioning by narrowing their goals (selection), concentrating practice on what matters (optimization), and finding alternative ways of doing things (compensation).

Key people

  • Erik Erikson 1902–1994
  • Paul Baltes 1939–2006

Key concepts

  • MultidirectionalityDifferent abilities change in different directions across life. Some rise while others fall.
