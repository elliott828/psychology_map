Trauma Studies and PTSD

School·1980·创伤研究与 PTSD

Extreme threatening experiences can leave lasting symptoms: intrusive memories, avoidance, hypervigilance and emotional numbing. Recognizing this as a real disorder changed the situation of victims of war and violence.

Historical influence ●●●●○·Evidence: Strong evidence·Public familiarity ●●●●○

Thread
Clinical and Mental Health
Landmark
DSM-III includes post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD)
What the evidence says
Trauma-focused cognitive behavioral therapy and similar treatments have much evidence. Some popular trauma narratives, such as 'the body remembers everything,' go beyond the evidence.
Key figure
Judith Herman

Core theories

  • Prolonged Exposure 1986In a safe therapy setting, the client repeatedly recounts the trauma and faces the memories and the situations they have been avoiding. This continues until fear decreases and the memory is reintegrated.
  • Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing 1989While recalling the trauma, the client follows the therapist's finger with side-to-side eye movements. This gradually 'desensitizes' the traumatic memory.
  • Three-Stage Model of Trauma Recovery 1992Recovery has three steps: establishing safety and stability, remembering and mourning the trauma, and reconnecting with life and other people.
  • Cognitive Processing Therapy 1992After trauma, people often hold thoughts such as 'it was my fault' or 'the world is not safe'. The therapy helps the patient identify these thoughts and revise them.
  • The Body Keeps the Score 1994Trauma is not only a matter of thoughts. It also stays in the body, showing up as hypervigilance, numbness, and physical symptoms. So treatment needs to attend to bodily sensations.
  • Post-Traumatic Growth 1996Some people who have gone through serious trauma report positive changes in their relationships, their values, and the meaning of their lives.
