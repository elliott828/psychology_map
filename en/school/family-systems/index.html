Family Systems Therapy

School·1956·家庭系统治疗

A person's symptoms are often an expression of the family as a whole. Changing the interaction patterns is more effective than treating the identified patient alone.

Historical influence ●●●○○·Evidence: Supported, with limits·Public familiarity ●●●○○

Thread
Clinical and Mental Health
Landmark
Bateson and colleagues publish the 'double bind' theory, which understands symptoms within family communication patterns
What the evidence says
Family interventions for adolescent problems (substance use, conduct problems, eating disorders) have strong evidence. The theories of the individual schools have had little testing.
Key figure
Gregory Bateson

Core theories

  • Double Bind Theory 1956A child who repeatedly receives two contradictory messages, and can neither avoid them nor comment on them, is caught in a bind.
  • Bowen Family Systems Theory 1966Anxiety is passed down through families. People with low levels of self-differentiation are easily pulled into emotional triangles and pass conflict on to the next generation.
  • Expressed Emotion 1972When family members are highly critical, hostile, or overinvolved with a patient, the patient is more likely to relapse. Teaching families to reduce these behaviors helps prevent relapse.
  • Structural Family Therapy 1974A family is made up of boundaries, alliances, and hierarchy. Symptoms often come from structural imbalance. The therapist adjusts the structure by changing seating, alliances, and similar elements.

Key concepts

  • Differentiation of SelfStaying true to your own thoughts and positions while remaining close to others.
  • TriangulationWhen two people are tense, a third person is pulled in to ease it.
