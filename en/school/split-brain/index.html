Split-Brain Research

School·1962·裂脑研究

When the corpus callosum is cut, the two hemispheres can barely exchange information, and the patient's left and right hands can 'do their own thing.' This was the first direct view of the division of labor between the hemispheres.

Historical influence ●●●○○·Evidence: Supported, with limits·Public familiarity ●●●●○

Thread
Brain and Biology
Landmark
Gazzaniga, Bogen and Sperry publish the first reports on patients with severed corpus callosums
What the evidence says
Experimental results from split-brain patients are reliable; extending them into a 'left brain rational, right brain emotional' personality type has no evidence.
Key figure
Michael Gazzaniga

Core theories

  • Hemispheric Specialization 1968For most people, language is handled mainly by the left hemisphere, while spatial processing and face processing rely more on the right. This is a tendency, not a division of 'thinking styles'.
  • Left-Brain Interpreter 1978In split-brain patients, the left hemisphere makes up a plausible-sounding reason for behavior that the right hemisphere initiated. People may often be giving themselves reasons after the fact.

Key people

Key concepts

  • LateralizationThe two hemispheres each lean toward certain functions.
  • Post-hoc RationalizationMaking up a plausible-sounding reason for an action that has already happened.
