Attachment Theory

School·1958·依恋理论

Infants are born needing a close bond with caregivers, from which they gain a sense of security. The quality of early care forms expectations about relationships, which shape how a person relates to others later.

Historical influence ●●●●○·Evidence: Strong evidence·Public familiarity ●●●●●

Thread
Lifespan Development
Landmark
Bowlby's The Nature of the Child's Tie to His Mother
What the evidence says
Types of infant attachment are moderately linked to adult relationships. The claim that 'attachment type determines a whole life' lacks support.
Key figure
John Bowlby

Core theories

  • Contact Comfort 1958Infant monkeys preferred clinging to a soft 'cloth mother' over a wire 'mother' that provided food. This shows attachment is not only about feeding.
  • Attachment Behavioral System 1969Infants are born with a set of behaviors that seek closeness to a caregiver. They are activated by threat, in order to gain protection and comfort. The caregiver's responses shape the infant's internal expectations about relationships.
  • Strange Situation 1970One-year-olds are separated from their mothers and reunited in a laboratory. Their reactions are observed, and attachment is classified as secure, avoidant, or ambivalent. Disorganized attachment was added later.
  • Adult Attachment 1987Adults also have attachment patterns in close relationships. Some people readily worry about being abandoned (anxiety), while others are reluctant to depend on others (avoidance).
