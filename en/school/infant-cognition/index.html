Infant Cognition

School·1985·婴儿认知研究

Uses how long infants look at something to infer what they understand: scenes that surprise them are looked at longer. The results suggest that infants have some basic knowledge earlier than Piaget believed.

Historical influence ●●●○○·Evidence: Supported, with limits·Public familiarity ●●○○○

Thread
Lifespan Development
Landmark
Baillargeon and colleagues' experiments: five-month-olds appear to 'know' that a hidden object still exists
What the evidence says
There are many findings on infants' early understanding of objects, number and other people, but the interpretation of looking-time measures and the replicability of some conclusions are still debated.
Key figure
Renee Baillargeon

Core theories

  • Neonatal Imitation 1977Newborns a few days old stick out their tongues when they see an adult do it. This has been taken as evidence that humans are born with a tendency to imitate.
  • Core Knowledge 1990Infants are born with a small number of basic intuitions about objects, number, space, and other people. These serve as the starting point for later learning.
  • Infant Statistical Learning 1996After listening to a few minutes of continuous speech, infants can detect which syllables always go together. This helps them pick out words from a stream of sound.
  • Infants as Scientists 1999Infants learn by observing, experimenting, picking up statistical regularities, and imitating. They act like little scientists, forming hypotheses and adjusting them based on evidence.
