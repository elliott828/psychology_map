Psychophysics

School·1860·心理物理学

Studies the quantitative relationship between physical stimuli (light, sound, weight) and subjective sensation, for example how much extra weight you need before you notice that something feels 'a bit heavier.'

Historical influence ●●●○○·Evidence: Strong evidence·Public familiarity ●○○○○

Thread
Mind and Cognition
Landmark
Fechner's Elements of Psychophysics turns the relationship between stimulus intensity and sensation into a measurable law
What the evidence says
Threshold measurement and the stimulus-sensation function are repeatable basic methods, still used today in perception, medicine and engineering.
Key figure
Gustav Fechner

Core theories

  • Weber-Fechner Law 1860A stimulus must increase by a fixed proportion before a person notices a change. In a noisy room, you have to raise your voice a lot before anyone hears you.
