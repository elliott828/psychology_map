Moral Development

School·1958·道德发展

How people judge right and wrong changes with age: from avoiding punishment and following rules, to reasoning from universal principles.

Historical influence ●●●○○·Evidence: Mixed findings·Public familiarity ●●○○○

Thread
Lifespan Development
Landmark
Kohlberg's doctoral dissertation proposes stages in the development of moral judgment
What the evidence says
The trend of moral reasoning maturing with age is confirmed. The universality of the stages, and their applicability to women and to non-Western populations, have been questioned.
Key figure
Lawrence Kohlberg

Core theories

  • Kohlberg's Six Stages 1969Moral reasoning develops step by step from low to high: from 'avoid punishment' and 'obey the rules', to 'uphold the social contract' and 'follow universal ethical principles'.
  • Ethic of Care 1982Morality is not only about fairness and rights. It is also about relationships and responsibility. Kohlberg's system emphasizes the former and so underrates the moral maturity of many women.

Key people

  • Lawrence Kohlberg 1927–1987
  • Carol Gilligan 1936–

Key concepts

  • Moral DilemmaA hypothetical situation with no simple right answer, used to observe how people reason.

Relations to other schools

  • ← Develops Cognitive Development TheoryKohlberg extended Piaget's study of moral judgment into a stage theory.
