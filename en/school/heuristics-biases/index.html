Heuristics and Biases

School·1974·启发式与偏差

When making judgments, people use labor-saving shortcuts. These usually work well, but in specific situations they systematically go wrong, for example by anchoring on an irrelevant number.

Historical influence ●●●●○·Evidence: Supported, with limits·Public familiarity ●●●●○

Thread
Mind and Cognition
Landmark
Tversky and Kahneman publish 'Judgment under Uncertainty: Heuristics and Biases' in Science
What the evidence says
Most bias phenomena replicate. The explanation that 'people are irrational' has been criticized, and some later effects, such as certain priming effects, have not held up.
Key figure
Daniel Kahneman

Core theories

  • Prospect Theory 1979People judge gains and losses not by final absolute amounts, but by change relative to a reference point. An equal-sized loss hurts more than a gain feels good.
  • Ecological Rationality 1999Heuristics are not flaws. When a rule matches the structure of the environment, a simple shortcut is often more accurate than a complex calculation.
  • Dual-Process Theory 2011Thinking has a fast, automatic mode (System 1) and a slow, effortful mode (System 2). Many errors come from System 1 going unchecked by System 2.

Key concepts

  • Anchoring EffectA number heard first pulls later estimates off course.
  • Availability HeuristicThings that come to mind easily seem more common.
  • Loss AversionThe pain of losing is greater than the pleasure of gaining the same amount.
