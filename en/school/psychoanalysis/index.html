Classical Psychoanalysis

School·1895·经典精神分析

Psychological symptoms come from repressed conflicts. Through free association, dream interpretation and analysis of transference, unconscious material is brought into awareness, reducing symptoms.

Historical influence ●●●●●·Evidence: Mixed findings·Public familiarity ●●●●●

Thread
Unconscious and Depth
Landmark
Breuer and Freud's Studies on Hysteria
What the evidence says
Unconscious processing, defense and the influence of early relationships have support. Core theories such as libido and the Oedipus complex are mostly untestable, and the evidence for its efficacy is limited but not zero.
Key figure
Sigmund Freud

Core theories

  • The Interpretation of Dreams 1900A dream is a disguised fulfillment of an unconscious wish. Interpreting a dream means tracing from its manifest content back to its latent content.
  • Drive Theory and Unconscious Conflict 1905People are driven by instincts for sex and aggression, which conflict with the demands of reality and morality. The conflict is pushed into the unconscious and then appears as symptoms, dreams, and slips of the tongue.
  • Oedipus Complex 1910Children around three to four are attracted to the opposite-sex parent and hostile toward the same-sex parent. How these conflicts are resolved determines later personality.
  • Transference 1912Patients bring feelings and expectations formed toward important people in early life into their relationship with the therapist. The therapist can use this to see the patient's patterns.
  • Repression 1915Painful material is pushed out of consciousness and stored in the unconscious. It may later return under suitable conditions.
  • Structural Model: Id, Ego, Superego 1923The mind is divided into the id, which seeks gratification; the ego, which takes reality into account; and the superego, which internalizes norms. Symptoms arise from conflict among the three.

Key concepts

  • The UnconsciousMental processes that are outside awareness but still shape behavior.

  • Free AssociationSay whatever comes to mind, without filtering.

  • → Branches Neo-Freudian SchoolHorney and Sullivan rejected libido theory and turned to culture and interpersonal relations.
  • → Develops Lacanian PsychoanalysisLacan claimed to 'return to Freud' and reread him through structural linguistics.
  • → Reacts Family Systems TherapyFamily therapists were dissatisfied with looking for causes only inside the individual mind.
  • → Reacts Cognitive TherapiesEllis and Beck were trained in psychoanalysis but set out separately, finding it hard to test.

  • → Reacts Person-Centered TherapyRejects the view that people are driven by drives and believes in an inherent tendency to grow.
  • → Develops Psychosocial DevelopmentErikson expanded psychosexual stages into lifelong psychosocial stages.
  • → Reacts Behavior TherapyEysenck and Wolpe criticized psychoanalysis for lacking evidence of efficacy and advocated treatment based on learning principles.
