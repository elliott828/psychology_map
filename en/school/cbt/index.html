Cognitive-Behavioral Therapy

School·1977·认知行为治疗的整合

Integrates behavioral techniques (exposure, behavioral activation) with cognitive techniques (testing thoughts) in a structured, time-limited, goal-oriented format.

Historical influence ●●●●●·Evidence: Strong evidence·Public familiarity ●●●●○

Thread
Clinical and Mental Health
Landmark
Meichenbaum's Cognitive-Behavior Modification
What the evidence says
In hundreds of controlled trials it has reliable efficacy for depression, anxiety, OCD and insomnia. The long-term maintenance of its effects and its mechanisms are still being studied.
Key figure
Donald Meichenbaum

Core theories

  • Exposure and Response Prevention 1966People with obsessive-compulsive disorder are exposed to the things that cause anxiety while not performing rituals such as handwashing. Anxiety naturally declines, and the compulsions weaken with it.
  • Cognitive-Behavior Modification 1977What people say to themselves affects their behavior. Learning more helpful 'inner dialogue' can improve impulsivity, anxiety, and stress coping.
  • Cognitive Model of Panic 1986A panic attack happens when bodily sensations, such as a racing heart, are misread as 'I am dying'. This triggers stronger anxiety and creates a vicious cycle.
  • Behavioral Activation 1996Depressed people stop doing things because they have no interest, which leaves them with fewer rewards in life. Acting first and scheduling meaningful activities leads mood to improve afterward.
