Classical Conditioning

School·1903·经典条件作用

A neutral signal (a bell), when repeatedly paired with an important event (food), can come to elicit the same response (salivation).

Historical influence ●●●●○·Evidence: Strong evidence·Public familiarity ●●●●○

Thread
Learning and Behavior
Landmark
Pavlov reports his research on conditioned reflexes at the International Medical Congress in Madrid
What the evidence says
Basic regularities of associative learning have been repeated across many species; modern theories emphasize 'prediction' rather than simple co-occurrence.
Key figure
Ivan Pavlov

Core theories

  • Conditioned Emotional Response (Little Albert) 1920A baby was startled by a loud noise when he saw a white rat. After a few pairings, he cried whenever he saw a rat. This is cited as proof that phobias can be learned, but the evidence behind it is much thinner than textbooks suggest.
  • Conditioned Reflex: Acquisition, Extinction, Generalization 1927After a conditioned stimulus is paired with an unconditioned stimulus, the conditioned stimulus alone can trigger the response. If the pairing stops for a long time, the response fades, but it may spontaneously recover after a while.
  • Taste Aversion and Biological Preparedness 1966If someone gets sick after eating a food, they can develop a lasting dislike for its taste, even when the illness comes hours later. Pairing light with nausea, by contrast, is hard to learn.
  • Rescorla-Wagner Model 1972Learning happens only when the outcome is unexpected. If a signal already fully predicts food, adding a second signal teaches almost nothing new.

Key concepts

  • Extinction and Spontaneous RecoveryOnce the pairing stops, the response fades, but it can return on its own.
  • Stimulus GeneralizationA learned response spreads to similar stimuli.
  • Prediction ErrorThe gap between outcome and expectation is what drives learning.
