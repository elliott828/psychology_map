Object Relations

School·1932·客体关系

The most fundamental human drive is to form relationships with others. Early experiences with caregivers are internalized as 'internal objects' and continue to shape later interpersonal patterns.

Historical influence ●●●●○·Evidence: Supported, with limits·Public familiarity ●●○○○

Thread
Unconscious and Depth
Landmark
Klein's The Psycho-Analysis of Children
What the evidence says
Support from many sources for early relationships shaping later interpersonal relations and self-image. Klein's specific developmental stages lack empirical evidence.
Key figure
Melanie Klein

Core theories

  • Internal Objects and Early Care 1935Infants form internal images of 'good' and 'bad' from their interactions with the mother. These internal objects shape how they perceive others later on.
  • Paranoid-Schizoid and Depressive Positions 1946Infants first divide the world into 'all good' and 'all bad' (splitting). Later they can see the good and bad in the same person at once, and feel guilt and concern.
  • Transitional Object 1953The blanket or toy an infant clings to is a bridge from 'being one with the mother' to 'the outside world'.
  • Holding Environment and the Good-Enough Mother 1960Infants need a stable environment. The mother does not have to be perfect, only 'good enough' in responding. Without this holding, a 'false self' tends to form.
