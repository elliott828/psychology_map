Behaviorism

School·1913·行为主义

Psychology should study observable, measurable behavior the way physics studies its subject matter. It should exclude introspection and 'consciousness' from science, and explain behavior as the result of environmental stimuli.

Historical influence ●●●●●·Evidence: Mostly historical·Public familiarity ●●●●○

Thread
Learning and Behavior
Landmark
Watson publishes 'Psychology as the Behaviorist Views It,' arguing that psychology should study only observable behavior
What the evidence says
Learning regularities have stood up to testing, but the program of 'studying behavior only, not the mind' has been replaced by cognitive research.
Key figure
John B. Watson

Core theories

  • Radical Behaviorism 1945Inner thoughts and feelings also count as behavior, only they are not visible. To explain behavior, you only need to find the antecedents and consequences in the environment, without assuming an inner 'entity' in the mind.

Key people

  • John B. Watson 1878–1958
  • B. F. Skinner 1904–1990

Key concepts

  • Stimulus-ResponseBehaviorism's basic unit for describing behavior: an external stimulus triggers an observable response.

Relations to other schools

  • ← Develops Law of EffectThorndike's animal experiments foreshadowed behaviorism.
  • ← Develops Classical ConditioningWatson took the conditioned reflex as the basic unit explaining all learning.
  • → Develops Operant ConditioningSkinner pushed behaviorism to its limit, analyzing only behavior and its consequences.
  • → Branches Neo-BehaviorismKeeps objective methods but adds internal variables such as purpose and expectation.
  • ← Critiques Taste Aversion and Biological PreparednessTaste aversion shows that learning has biological biases, so stimuli are not interchangeable.
  • ← Critiques Contact ComfortInfant monkeys cling to a cloth mother without feeding, refuting the idea that attachment comes from feeding reinforcement.
  • ← Critiques Overjustification EffectRewards do not always strengthen behavior; they can weaken original interest.
  • ← Reacts Experimental PsychologyOpposed introspection and argued for studying only observable behavior.
  • ← Develops FunctionalismThe focus on adaptation and function paved the way for the objective study of behavior.
  • → Reacts Cognitive RevolutionLanguage and thought cannot be explained by stimulus and response alone.
  • → Reacts Hierarchy of Needs and Self-ActualizationHumanistic psychology called itself the 'third force' and opposed behaviorism's mechanistic view.
