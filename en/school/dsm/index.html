DSM / ICD Diagnostic Systems

School·1952·DSM／ICD 诊断体系

A diagnostic manual that specifies 'what counts as a particular mental disorder,' used for clinical work, research, insurance and communication. It is also criticized for 'putting labels on suffering.'

Historical influence ●●●●●·Evidence: Mixed findings·Public familiarity ●●●●○

Thread
Clinical and Mental Health
Landmark
The first edition of the DSM is published; the turning point is DSM-III in 1980, which introduces operational diagnostic criteria
What the evidence says
Inter-rater reliability of diagnosis rose markedly after DSM-III. Whether these categories correspond to distinct causes remains debated, and comorbidity is common.
Key figure
Robert Spitzer

Core theories

  • Rosenhan Experiment 1973Eight people with no mental illness claimed to hear voices and were admitted to psychiatric hospitals. After admission they behaved normally but were not recognized as sane. It was used to show that diagnosis is unreliable.
  • DSM-III Operational Criteria 1980Each disorder is defined by how many symptoms are present and how long they last. This reduced disagreement between doctors, but it also made diagnosis more like a checklist.
