Operant Conditioning

School·1938·操作性条件作用

Behavior is shaped by its consequences: reinforced behavior increases, and behavior that is not reinforced or is punished decreases. Animal training, classroom management and game design all use this.

Historical influence ●●●●○·Evidence: Strong evidence·Public familiarity ●●●●○

Thread
Learning and Behavior
Landmark
Skinner's The Behavior of Organisms introduces operant behavior and reinforcement
What the evidence says
Regularities such as reinforcement, extinction and reinforcement schedules have much replicable evidence, but they are not enough to explain language and complex human thought.
Key figure
B. F. Skinner

Core theories

  • Reinforcement and Shaping 1953A complex behavior is broken into small steps, and each step closer to the goal is reinforced. This gradually shapes a new behavior, such as training pigeons to play table tennis.
  • Schedules of Reinforcement 1957Whether rewards come on a regular or random schedule produces different rhythms of behavior. Unpredictable rewards, as in slot machines, make it hardest for people to stop.
  • Matching Law 1961When animals distribute their time or responses between two options, the proportion roughly matches the proportion of rewards each option gives.

Key people

  • B. F. Skinner 1904–1990

Key concepts

  • Negative ReinforcementRemoving something unpleasant makes a behavior more frequent. It is not punishment.
  • Variable-Ratio ScheduleNot knowing which attempt will pay off is what keeps people hooked.
  • ShapingGradually rewarding small steps toward a goal.
  • Habit LoopCue, routine, reward: these three repeat until a habit forms.
