Cognitive Development Theory

School·1936·认知发展理论

Children do not passively receive knowledge. Through handling things, trying and failing, they actively build their understanding of the world, and the way they understand changes qualitatively.

Historical influence ●●●●○·Evidence: Supported, with limits·Public familiarity ●●●●○

Thread
Lifespan Development
Landmark
Piaget's The Origins of Intelligence in Children: intelligence is gradually constructed from sensorimotor activity
What the evidence says
The core idea that children actively construct knowledge is widely accepted. The strict stage divisions and the specific ages for 'what children can do at what age' have been revised by later research.
Key figure
Jean Piaget

Core theories

  • Piaget's Four Stages 1947A child's thinking passes through four stages in order. First they learn about the world through action, then they use symbols, then they can carry out concrete logical operations, and finally they can reason abstractly.

Key people

  • Jean Piaget 1896–1980

Key concepts

  • Assimilation and AccommodationUnderstanding something new through an existing framework, or changing the framework to fit it.
  • ConservationUnderstanding that the amount stays the same even when the shape changes.
  • Object PermanenceKnowing that a hidden object still exists.
  • EgocentrismDifficulty seeing things from another person's point of view.
