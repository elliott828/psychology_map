All concepts

Psychophysics

  • Just Noticeable DifferenceThe smallest change that can just barely be detected.

Cerebral Localization

  • AphasiaLoss of language ability after brain injury. Different areas produce different symptoms.

Individual Differences

  • Regression to the MeanAfter an extreme result, the next result is usually closer to average.
  • Correlation Is Not CausationTwo things changing together does not mean one causes the other.

Experimental Psychology

  • IntrospectionTrained participants report their immediate feelings and conscious contents.

Child Study Movement

  • Developmental MilestonesAbilities that most children acquire at roughly similar ages.

Experimental Study of Memory

  • Retrieval PracticeTrying to recall something on your own helps you learn it better than rereading.

Hypnosis, Hysteria and Dissociation

  • DissociationA separation of functions that normally integrate, such as consciousness, memory and identity.

Functionalism

  • The I and the MeJames split the self into the part that knows and the part that is known.

Neuron Doctrine

  • SynapseThe contact point where signals pass between two neurons.

Crowd Psychology

  • DeindividuationIn a group, individual self-awareness and sense of responsibility decline.

Classical Psychoanalysis

  • The UnconsciousMental processes that are outside awareness but still shape behavior.
  • ProjectionAttributing your own emotions or traits to other people.
  • Free AssociationSay whatever comes to mind, without filtering.

Birth of Clinical Psychology

  • Case FormulationA therapist organizes a client's problems, their causes and what maintains them into a testable explanation.

Law of Effect

  • Trial-and-Error LearningAnimals gradually keep the actions that bring good results, through repeated attempts.

Modern Psychiatric Classification

  • Categorical DiagnosisDividing mental problems into discrete categories of “present” or “absent.”

Classical Conditioning

  • Extinction and Spontaneous RecoveryOnce the pairing stops, the response fades, but it can return on its own.
  • Stimulus GeneralizationA learned response spreads to similar stimuli.
  • Prediction ErrorThe gap between outcome and expectation is what drives learning.

General Intelligence and Factor Analysis

  • Positive ManifoldScores on different intelligence tests are almost always positively correlated with one another.

Intelligence Testing

  • Mental AgeMatching a child's test performance to the typical level of children of a given age.

Gestalt Psychology

  • Figure-GroundPerception always treats some parts as the “figure” and the rest as the “ground.”

Individual Psychology

  • Inferiority ComplexFeelings of inadequacy dominate life, showing up as withdrawal or bravado.

Analytical Psychology

  • ArchetypeTypical figures that recur in dreams and myths.
  • ShadowThe side of yourself you refuse to admit.
  • PersonaThe role you present to others in society.
  • Anima and AnimusThe image of the feminine in a man's psyche, and of the masculine in a woman's.
  • IndividuationThe process of becoming a whole, unique self.
  • ComplexA cluster of unconscious feelings and memories built around an emotional experience.
  • Introversion and ExtraversionWhether energy is directed more inward or toward the outside world.

Behaviorism

  • Stimulus-ResponseBehaviorism's basic unit for describing behavior: an external stimulus triggers an observable response.

Morita Therapy

  • Psychic Interaction (Seishin Kōgo Sayō)A cycle in which attention focused on discomfort makes the discomfort grow stronger.

Neo-Behaviorism

  • Intervening VariableInternal processes, such as expectations and purposes, that Tolman placed between stimulus and response.

Object Relations

  • Good-Enough MotherNo need to be perfect, only reliable enough.

Schema and Reconstructive Memory

  • False MemoryPeople can vividly “remember” things that never happened.

Sociocultural Theory

  • Private SpeechChildren talk to themselves while doing things, to help them think and regulate their behavior.

Ethology

  • Critical / Sensitive PeriodSome abilities form most easily during specific stages of development.
  • ImprintingYoung animals quickly identify and follow the first object they encounter.

Cognitive Development Theory

  • Assimilation and AccommodationUnderstanding something new through an existing framework, or changing the framework to fit it.
  • ConservationUnderstanding that the amount stays the same even when the shape changes.
  • Object PermanenceKnowing that a hidden object still exists.
  • EgocentrismDifficulty seeing things from another person's point of view.

Ego Psychology

  • Defense MechanismMental strategies people use, often without awareness, to reduce anxiety.

Field Theory and Group Dynamics

  • GroupthinkIn the pursuit of unity, a group stops rigorously testing its decisions.

Stress Physiology

  • AllostasisThe body copes with change by shifting its “set point.”

Neo-Freudian School

  • Idealized SelfA perfect image a person builds to cope with inner insecurity.

Trait Theory

  • Barnum EffectPeople are willing to believe vague descriptions that seem “spot on” for them.
  • Trait vs. StateStable tendencies must be distinguished from momentary states.

Operant Conditioning

  • Negative ReinforcementRemoving something unpleasant makes a behavior more frequent. It is not punishment.
  • Variable-Ratio ScheduleNot knowing which attempt will pay off is what keeps people hooked.
  • ShapingGradually rewarding small steps toward a goal.
  • Habit LoopCue, routine, reward: these three repeat until a habit forms.

Hierarchy of Needs and Self-Actualization

  • Peak ExperienceA brief, intense moment of meaningful experience.

Logotherapy

  • Meaning in LifeFeeling that life has purpose, matters and makes sense.

Factor-Analytic Personality Models

  • NeuroticismA tendency to readily experience negative emotions such as anxiety, anger and low mood.

Hebbian Learning and Plasticity

  • EngramThe physical trace a specific memory leaves in the brain.
  • NeuroplasticityThe brain changes its connections and structure in response to experience.
  • Synaptic StrengtheningA cellular phenomenon in which connections grow stronger with use.

Post-Jungian and Archetypal Psychology

  • Ego-Self AxisThe connection between the ego (the center of consciousness) and the Self (the whole psyche).

Psychosocial Development

  • Identity CrisisA period when the question “Who am I?” becomes especially pressing.

Conformity and Obedience

  • Diffusion of ResponsibilityThe more people present, the less responsible each person feels.
  • Social ProofLooking at what others do to decide what you should do.
  • Foot-in-the-DoorAgreeing to a small request makes it easier to agree to a larger one.

Gestalt Therapy

  • Empty ChairSpeaking to an empty chair as if a person were sitting in it, to say what was left unsaid.

Person-Centered Therapy

  • Unconditional Positive RegardAcceptance that does not depend on how you perform.

DSM / ICD Diagnostic Systems

  • ComorbidityOne person meets criteria for more than one diagnosis at the same time.

Psychopharmacology

  • Chemical Imbalance IdeaThe idea that depression and other mood disorders are caused by a shortage of brain chemicals.

Lacanian Psychoanalysis

  • Imaginary, Symbolic, RealLacan divided the psychological world into three intertwined registers.

Prejudice Research

  • ScapegoatingBlaming and venting frustrations, one's own or a group's, on a vulnerable group.

Cognitive Therapies

  • Cognitive DistortionCommon biased habits of thinking about problems.
  • RuminationChewing over the same problem repeatedly without making progress.

Test Theory (Reliability and Validity)

  • ReliabilityHow stable a measurement is.
  • ValidityWhether a test measures what it is meant to measure.
  • Social DesirabilityWhen answering questionnaires, people tend to give answers that make them look good.

Cognitive Revolution

  • Information-Processing MetaphorTreating the mind as a system that takes in, encodes, stores and retrieves information.

Family Systems Therapy

  • Differentiation of SelfStaying true to your own thoughts and positions while remaining close to others.
  • TriangulationWhen two people are tense, a third person is pulled in to ease it.

Cognitive Dissonance

  • Effort JustificationThe more effort put into something, the more people tend to think it is worth having.

Neuropsychology

  • Double DissociationTwo opposite types of damage show that two functions are independent of each other.

Attachment Theory

  • Secure BaseA child uses the caregiver as a starting point for exploring.
  • Attachment StylesSeveral common ways of relating intimately, from infancy into adult relationships.

Attribution Theory

  • Self-Serving BiasCredit success to yourself, blame failure on circumstances.

Behavior Therapy

  • CounterconditioningRepeatedly pairing a fear-provoking stimulus with a response that is opposite to fear, such as relaxation.

Existential Psychotherapy

  • Existential GivensDeath, freedom, isolation and meaninglessness: four things no one can avoid.

Moral Development

  • Moral DilemmaA hypothetical situation with no simple right answer, used to observe how people reason.

Behavior Genetics

  • HeritabilityIn a population, how much of the variation in a trait is linked to genetic differences.

Common Factors

  • Therapeutic AllianceThe relationship in which therapist and client work together.

Social Learning Theory

  • ModelingPeople watch what others do and then copy it.

Split-Brain Research

  • LateralizationThe two hemispheres each lean toward certain functions.
  • Post-hoc RationalizationMaking up a plausible-sounding reason for an action that has already happened.

Cognitive Psychology

  • Working MemoryA limited capacity to hold and work with several things at once.
  • Inattentional BlindnessFocusing on one thing can make you miss other obvious things.
  • Confirmation BiasA tendency to look for evidence that supports what we already think.
  • PrimingEncountering one stimulus shapes how related content is processed afterward.

Learned Helplessness

  • ControllabilityThe experience that “nothing I do makes a difference” is key to learned helplessness.

Applied Behavior Analysis

  • Behavioral ContractGoals and rewards are written down as an agreement in advance.

Person-Situation Debate

  • Cross-Situational ConsistencyHow consistent a person's behavior is across different settings.

Molecular Basis of Memory

  • Memory ConsolidationNew memories take time to move from fragile to stable.

Indigenous Psychology

  • GuanxiNetworks of personal connection in Chinese society, held together by reciprocity and obligation.

Self Psychology

  • MirroringCaregivers “see” the child with admiration and responsiveness, so the child feels valued.

Heuristics and Biases

  • Anchoring EffectA number heard first pulls later estimates off course.
  • Availability HeuristicThings that come to mind easily seem more common.
  • Loss AversionThe pain of losing is greater than the pleasure of gaining the same amount.

Flow

  • Autotelic ActivityThe activity is its own reward.

Biopsychosocial Model

  • Predisposing, Precipitating and Perpetuating FactorsWhen analyzing a problem, causes are sorted into three categories by timing.

Cognitive-Behavioral Therapy

  • ExposureFacing what you fear, under safe conditions.
  • Avoidance and Safety BehaviorsRelieves anxiety in the short term, but keeps fear alive in the long term.

Cognitive Neuroscience

  • Binding ProblemColor, shape and motion are processed separately. Why do we see a unified object?

Ecological Systems Theory

  • TemperamentInnate styles of reacting, such as being easily aroused or easily calmed.

Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction

  • MindfulnessConsciously paying attention to the present moment, without judgment.

Social Identity Theory

  • In-Group BiasA preference for members of the group you belong to.

Life-Span Developmental Psychology

  • MultidirectionalityDifferent abilities change in different directions across life. Some rise while others fall.

Trauma Studies and PTSD

  • Post-Traumatic Stress ReactionsLasting reactions that follow an extreme event.

Multiple Intelligences

  • Learning StylesClaims like “I am a visual learner” lack supporting evidence.

Relational Psychoanalysis

  • EnactmentTherapist and client unknowingly act out the old relationship patterns of both.

Theory of Mind

  • False BeliefUnderstanding that what others believe may differ from the facts.

Social Cognition

  • StereotypeA simplified generalization about a category of people.
  • Self-Fulfilling ProphecyBecause you believe something will happen, you make it happen.

Big Five Personality

  • Openness to ExperienceInterest in and acceptance of new ideas, art and experiences.

Infant Cognition

  • Violation-of-ExpectationInfants who look longer at “impossible” events reveal what they expect.

Self-Determination Theory

  • Intrinsic MotivationDoing something because the activity itself is interesting.

Connectionism

  • Distributed RepresentationA concept is not stored in any single unit. It is represented by the pattern of activity across many units.

Emotion-Focused Therapy

  • Pursue-Withdraw CycleOne partner pursues for a response, the other withdraws, and each reinforces the other in a vicious cycle.

Cultural Psychology

  • Individualism vs. CollectivismWhether a society emphasizes the individual or the group.

Embodied Cognition

  • Extended MindCognitive processes can extend into tools and the environment outside the body.

Mentalization

  • MentalizingUnderstanding your own and others' behavior as driven by thoughts, feelings and intentions.

Third-Wave Therapies

  • Psychological FlexibilityBeing able to act on your values even when it is uncomfortable.
  • Radical AcceptanceFully acknowledging that reality is what it is, without approving of it.

Evolutionary Psychology

  • Evolutionary MismatchAdaptations to ancestral environments can become problems in modern ones.

Affective Neuroscience

  • Limbic SystemA collective name for brain structures involved in emotion, motivation and memory.

Evidence-Based Practice in Psychology

  • Randomized Controlled TrialRandomly assigning participants to a treatment group and a control group, then comparing outcomes.

Reward Prediction Error

  • Wanting versus LikingThe drive to want something and the pleasure of enjoying it rely on different brain systems.

Positive Psychology

  • Hedonic vs. Eudaimonic Well-BeingWell-being comes in two kinds: feeling good and living meaningfully.

Neuropsychoanalysis

  • Affective ConsciousnessFeeling itself is the most basic form of consciousness, arising from deep in the brainstem.

Self-Compassion

  • Common HumanityRecognizing that pain and failure are experiences everyone encounters.

Replication Crisis and Open Science

  • Effect SizeHow large an effect is, not just whether it exists.
  • PreregistrationWriting down hypotheses and the analysis plan before doing the study.
