Aphasia

Concept·失语症

Loss of language ability after brain injury. Different areas produce different symptoms.

School
Cerebral Localization
In plain words
After damage to the language areas of the brain (usually in the left hemisphere), a person may have trouble speaking, understanding speech, repeating words or finding words. Intelligence and the vocal organs themselves can be intact.
Example
After a stroke, one person can understand family members but can only say scattered words. Another speaks fluently, but what they say makes no coherent sense.
Common misuse
Aphasia is not “becoming foolish” or “damage to the voice box.” Most people with aphasia keep their thinking abilities. The problem lies in producing or understanding language.
