Idealized Self

Concept·理想化自我

A perfect image a person builds to cope with inner insecurity.

School
Neo-Freudian School
In plain words
Horney believed that when people feel unaccepted, they construct an image of how they “should be,” and then demand that the real self meet it harshly. This only intensifies inner conflict.
Example
Someone who insists they “must be flawless” falls into self-blame over the slightest mistake.
Common misuse
Treating it as “narcissism” or “self-confidence.” The idealized self is a defense against insecurity and is often accompanied by intense self-criticism.
