Post-hoc Rationalization

Concept·事后合理化

Making up a plausible-sounding reason for an action that has already happened.

School
Split-Brain Research
In plain words
When people do not know the real cause of their behavior, they automatically construct an explanation that sounds reasonable, and they sincerely believe it.
Example
In split-brain experiments, the right hemisphere saw the word “walk,” and the patient stood up and walked. The left hemisphere then said, “I wanted to go get a Coke.”
Common misuse
This does not mean “people are always lying.” It describes how limited our understanding of our own motives is to begin with.
