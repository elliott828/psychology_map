Imaginary, Symbolic, Real

Concept·想象界、象征界、实在界

Lacan divided the psychological world into three intertwined registers.

School
Lacanian Psychoanalysis
In plain words
The imaginary is the world of images and mirror reflections. The symbolic is language, rules and one's place among others. The real is the part that cannot be fully captured in language.
Example
A baby recognizing themselves in a mirror belongs to the imaginary. Learning to refer to oneself as “I” is entering the symbolic.
Common misuse
Treating these as operational definitions in psychology. They are philosophical concepts within psychoanalytic theory and do not correspond to directly measurable variables.
