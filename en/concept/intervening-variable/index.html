Intervening Variable

Concept·中介变量

Internal processes, such as expectations and purposes, that Tolman placed between stimulus and response.

School
Neo-Behaviorism
In plain words
Given the same stimulus, different animals respond differently, because between stimulus and response there are internal states like “expectations” and “cognitive maps.” These states can be defined and tested through experimental conditions.
Example
A rat in a maze finds the usual exit blocked and immediately takes another route, which suggests it has a map of the whole maze in its head.
Common misuse
Thinking that an intervening variable is a “mental entity.” Tolman used it as a measurable, operationally defined construct, not as an assumption about the soul.
