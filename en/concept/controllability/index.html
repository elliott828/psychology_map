Controllability

Concept·可控性

The experience that “nothing I do makes a difference” is key to learned helplessness.

School
Learned Helplessness
In plain words
In Seligman's experiments, dogs that had been forced to endure inescapable shocks later did not try to escape even when escape was possible. What mattered was the lack of control, not the shock itself.
Example
An employee who has been criticized for years and never earns recognition, however hard they try, gradually stops volunteering ideas.
Common misuse
Treating helplessness as a sign of weak character. Later research suggests that passivity is the default response, and that learning “I can control this” takes experience.
