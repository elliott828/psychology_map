Individual Differences and Measurement

Thread

Why are people different, and how can we measure it?

Schools (by date)

  • Individual Differences 1869Studies not only general laws of human beings but also the differences between people, and invents statistical methods for measuring those differences.
  • General Intelligence and Factor Analysis 1904Scores on different intelligence tests are positively correlated, which suggests a common 'general ability' may lie behind them. Factor analysis is the statistical method used to find it.
  • Intelligence Testing 1905Uses standardized tasks to measure cognitive ability and converts the results into comparable scores (IQ). The original purpose was to help schools identify children who needed extra support.
  • Trait Theory 1937Sees personality as a set of relatively stable tendencies: some people are always extraverted, anxious or conscientious, and these can be measured and compared.
  • Factor-Analytic Personality Models 1947Uses factor analysis to distill a few basic dimensions from large numbers of personality descriptions. Eysenck proposed two or three, and Cattell sixteen.
  • Test Theory (Reliability and Validity) 1955Whether a test is usable depends on answering two questions: is it consistent (reliability), and does it measure what it is meant to measure (validity)?
  • Person-Situation Debate 1968How consistent is a person's behavior across different situations? Mischel argued that traits predict individual behaviors very weakly, which set off a debate lasting more than a decade.
  • Multiple Intelligences 1983Intelligence is not one thing. Language, logic-mathematics, spatial, musical, bodily-kinesthetic, interpersonal and intrapersonal abilities are distinct, but schools value only one or two of them.
  • Big Five Personality 1985Most personality differences can be described with five dimensions: openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness and neuroticism. Each dimension is continuous, and people fall at different points along it.
  • Replication Crisis and Open Science 2011Many published psychology findings fail to hold up in replication. Causes include small samples, flexible data analysis and publishing only positive results. Reforms include preregistration, open data and large-sample collaboration.
  • Digital Footprints and Personality Prediction 2013Likes, text and location that people leave online can be used to estimate traits such as the Big Five, which takes personality measurement beyond questionnaires.
  • Network Psychometrics 2013Does not treat symptoms as expressions of a single underlying 'disease' but as a network in which they trigger one another: insomnia causes fatigue, fatigue causes low mood, and low mood causes insomnia.
  • Large-Scale Replication Projects 2015Many labs use the same protocol and large samples to each replicate the same famous result, turning 'can it be replicated?' into a question that can be answered statistically.
