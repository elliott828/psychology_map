Unconscious and Depth

Thread

How does the mind beyond consciousness operate?

Schools (by date)

  • Hypnosis, Hysteria and Dissociation 1889Consciousness is not always unified. In hypnosis, hysteria or after intense trauma, part of experience can split off from consciousness and operate independently.
  • Classical Psychoanalysis 1895Psychological symptoms come from repressed conflicts. Through free association, dream interpretation and analysis of transference, unconscious material is brought into awareness, reducing symptoms.
  • Individual Psychology 1912A person is a goal-oriented whole. The core drives are overcoming inferiority, striving for superiority and integrating into community; neurosis is avoidance of life's tasks.
  • Analytical Psychology 1913Beyond the unconscious left by personal experience, there is a shared human 'collective unconscious,' whose archetypes (mother, hero, shadow) shape dreams and myths. The task of life is individuation: becoming a whole self.
  • Object Relations 1932The most fundamental human drive is to form relationships with others. Early experiences with caregivers are internalized as 'internal objects' and continue to shape later interpersonal patterns.
  • Ego Psychology 1936Focuses on how the ego regulates conflicts among instinct, reality and conscience, using defense mechanisms to protect the self. Adaptation becomes the focus of analysis.
  • Neo-Freudian School 1937Keeps the view of unconscious conflict but holds that culture and interpersonal relationships matter more than sexual instincts, as in Horney's 'basic anxiety.'
  • Post-Jungian and Archetypal Psychology 1949Developments after Jung: Neumann and Fordham studied the development of consciousness and the ego; Hillman developed an image-centered archetypal psychology. There is also sandplay and cross-cultural application.
  • Lacanian Psychoanalysis 1953The unconscious is not a storehouse of instincts but is structured like a language. A person becomes a subject within the discourse of the 'Other.'
  • Self Psychology 1971Whether a person has a stable sense of self-worth depends on whether caregivers saw them and mirrored and idealized them in early life. The core of treatment is empathic understanding.
  • Relational Psychoanalysis 1983Analysis is not a doctor objectively observing a patient but an interaction between two people. The therapist's own feelings and reactions are part of the treatment.
  • Mentalization 1991The ability to understand one's own and others' behavior in terms of thoughts, feelings and wishes. This ability grows out of early experiences of being understood; when it is lacking, emotional dysregulation and relationship difficulties tend to follow.
  • Neuropsychoanalysis 2000Uses case studies of brain damage and imaging research to test and revise psychoanalytic concepts such as dreams, the ego and emotion.
  • Evidence for Psychodynamic Therapy 2010Uses randomized controlled trials and meta-analyses to test long-term and short-term psychodynamic therapy. It finds effects similar to other therapies, and gains that continue to grow after treatment ends.
