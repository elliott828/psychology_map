Mind and Cognition

Thread

How does the mind process the world?

Schools (by date)

  • Psychophysics 1860Studies the quantitative relationship between physical stimuli (light, sound, weight) and subjective sensation, for example how much extra weight you need before you notice that something feels 'a bit heavier.'
  • Experimental Psychology 1879Studies mental processes through experiments with controlled conditions and repeated measurement, and trains observers to report their own sensations (introspection).
  • Experimental Study of Memory 1885Brings even higher mental processes into the lab: using himself as the subject, Ebbinghaus measured how fast he forgot after learning a list of nonsense syllables.
  • Functionalism 1890Rather than asking what the mind is made of, it asks what the mind does and how it helps survival. It also urges psychology to respond to practical problems such as education and work.
  • Gestalt Psychology 1912What we see is a whole, not a sum of parts: some dots automatically form a figure based on proximity and similarity, and the moment of solving a problem can be a reorganization of the whole.
  • Schema and Reconstructive Memory 1932Memory is not a matter of playing back a recording of the past. Instead, existing knowledge frameworks (schemas) rebuild it at recall, which is why memories become distorted.
  • Cognitive Revolution 1956Using the computer as an analogy, it sees the mind as a system that takes in, stores and transforms information, making 'internal processes' a legitimate object of scientific study again.
  • Cognitive Psychology 1967An experimental discipline that studies how perception, attention, memory, language and thinking work. It has become the backbone of modern psychology.
  • Heuristics and Biases 1974When making judgments, people use labor-saving shortcuts. These usually work well, but in specific situations they systematically go wrong, for example by anchoring on an irrelevant number.
  • Connectionism 1986Uses networks of many simple connected units to model the mind. Knowledge is not stored in one place but distributed across the strengths of connections, adjusted through learning.
  • Embodied Cognition 1991The mind is not only in the brain. It is formed by the brain, the body and the environment together, and thinking often relies on bodily movement and space.
  • Predictive Processing 2010The brain does not passively receive signals. It continually predicts what it will feel next, and adjusts only when prediction and input differ.
  • Deep Learning as Cognitive Models 2012Treats deep neural networks as candidate models of abilities such as vision and language, and compares them with brain activity and behavioral data.
  • Large Language Models and Cognition 2022On one hand, uses psychological tasks to test whether language models have abilities such as reasoning and theory of mind. On the other hand, uses them to generate materials, simulate participants and analyze text.
