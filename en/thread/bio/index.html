Brain and Biology

Thread

How does the mind arise from the body and from evolution?

Schools (by date)

  • Cerebral Localization 1861Different mental functions depend on different parts of the brain. The starting point was a patient who could only say the syllable 'Tan.' After his death, an autopsy showed damage to the left frontal lobe.
  • Neuron Doctrine 1891The nervous system is not a continuous net, but is made of separate nerve cells (neurons) that pass signals to one another at points of contact.
  • Ethology 1935Studies animal behavior in natural settings, treating instincts and imprinting as innate tendencies shaped by evolution, and asks what they are for and where they come from.
  • Stress Physiology 1936When the body faces a threat, it triggers a hormonal response. If the response stays on for a long time, it damages the immune system, the cardiovascular system and the brain.
  • Hebbian Learning and Plasticity 1949Learning means changes in the connections between neurons: cells that often fire together become more strongly connected. This became a basic rule for later neural network models.
  • Psychopharmacology 1952Uses drugs to change neurotransmitter systems and relieve psychiatric symptoms. It allowed many patients to leave long-term hospital care, but it also left behind the oversimplified claim that mental illness is a chemical imbalance in the brain.
  • Neuropsychology 1957By studying what brain-damaged patients lose, it infers which brain regions normal mental functions depend on, and it also develops clinical tools for assessing brain injury.
  • Behavior Genetics 1960Uses twins, adoptive families and genetic data to estimate how much of the difference in a trait comes from genes and how much from the environment.
  • Split-Brain Research 1962When the corpus callosum is cut, the two hemispheres can barely exchange information, and the patient's left and right hands can 'do their own thing.' This was the first direct view of the division of labor between the hemispheres.
  • Molecular Basis of Memory 1970Breaks 'learning' down to the level of cells: simple learning changes chemical signals at synapses and protein synthesis, and long-term memory requires new proteins.
  • Cognitive Neuroscience 1979Combines the task designs of cognitive psychology with lesion studies, brain imaging and electrophysiology, asking how thinking, memory and attention are carried out in the brain.
  • Evolutionary Psychology 1992Treats psychological mechanisms as products of natural selection in the ancestral environment, and starts from the question 'what adaptive problem does it solve?' to propose testable hypotheses.
  • Affective Neuroscience 1994Studies how emotion, threat responses, bodily states and decision-making work in the brain. One of its core findings is that emotion is not the enemy of reason.
  • Reward Prediction Error 1997Dopamine neurons fire when a reward is unexpected and do not fire when a reward is fully predicted. This is exactly the 'prediction error' used in reinforcement learning algorithms.
  • Human Connectome 2010Rather than looking at brain regions one at a time, maps the connections across the whole brain as a graph and studies how patterns of connection relate to individual differences and disease.
  • Psychiatric Genomics 2014Uses genome data from hundreds of thousands of people to find sites associated with mental disorders. The conclusion is that these disorders are caused by hundreds or thousands of variants, each with a tiny effect.
