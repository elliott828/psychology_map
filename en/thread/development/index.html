Lifespan Development

Thread

How does a person become who they are?

Schools (by date)

  • Child Study Movement 1883From the late nineteenth century, researchers began systematically collecting data on children's development through questionnaires and observation. Children became a subject worth studying in their own right.
  • Sociocultural Theory 1934Children first accomplish things through interaction with others, then internalize those interactions as their own thinking. Language and symbols are tools of thought.
  • Cognitive Development Theory 1936Children do not passively receive knowledge. Through handling things, trying and failing, they actively build their understanding of the world, and the way they understand changes qualitatively.
  • Psychosocial Development 1950Each stage of life has a core psychological conflict, such as 'identity versus role confusion' in adolescence. How well it is handled affects later development.
  • Attachment Theory 1958Infants are born needing a close bond with caregivers, from which they gain a sense of security. The quality of early care forms expectations about relationships, which shape how a person relates to others later.
  • Moral Development 1958How people judge right and wrong changes with age: from avoiding punishment and following rules, to reasoning from universal principles.
  • Ecological Systems Theory 1979Children live in nested layers of environment: family, school, community policies and culture, and the historical era. Development is the result of the person's ongoing interaction with these environments.
  • Life-Span Developmental Psychology 1980Development does not stop at childhood or adolescence; it continues into old age, and includes both growth and decline. The interaction between the individual and the historical era cannot be ignored.
  • Theory of Mind 1983How people come to understand that others' minds differ from their own: knowing that others have their own beliefs and desires, and act on them.
  • Infant Cognition 1985Uses how long infants look at something to infer what they understand: scenes that surprise them are looked at longer. The results suggest that infants have some basic knowledge earlier than Piaget believed.
  • ABCD Study 2015Follows more than 10,000 nine- and ten-year-old children for ten years, repeatedly collecting brain imaging, cognitive tests and life surveys to see how the brain changes with experience.
  • Screens and Adolescent Mental Health 2017Depression and anxiety among adolescents have risen over the past decade. Is this related to smartphones and social media? One side points to time trends; the other notes that associations at the individual level are very small.
