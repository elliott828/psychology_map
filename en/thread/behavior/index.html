Learning and Behavior

Thread

How does experience shape behavior?

Schools (by date)

  • Law of Effect 1898The consequences of a behavior determine how likely it is to occur again: actions that satisfy an animal are kept, and those that cause discomfort are eliminated.
  • Classical Conditioning 1903A neutral signal (a bell), when repeatedly paired with an important event (food), can come to elicit the same response (salivation).
  • Behaviorism 1913Psychology should study observable, measurable behavior the way physics studies its subject matter. It should exclude introspection and 'consciousness' from science, and explain behavior as the result of environmental stimuli.
  • Neo-Behaviorism 1932Keeps the method of objective experiment but admits 'intervening variables' behind behavior: purpose, expectation and drive. Tolman and Hull are the two major figures.
  • Operant Conditioning 1938Behavior is shaped by its consequences: reinforced behavior increases, and behavior that is not reinforced or is punished decreases. Animal training, classroom management and game design all use this.
  • Behavior Therapy 1958Sees symptoms as learned responses and uses learning principles to change them: gradually facing the feared object in a safe setting until the fear fades.
  • Social Learning Theory 1961People can learn without being rewarded or punished themselves: they learn by observing a model's behavior and its consequences. Beliefs about whether one can succeed also shape action.
  • Learned Helplessness 1967After long exposure to negative events that cannot be controlled, people or animals stop trying, even when the environment later becomes controllable. It was once used to explain depression.
  • Applied Behavior Analysis 1968Uses functional analysis to identify the antecedents and consequences of behavior, then uses reinforcement, prompting and other means to change specific, measurable behaviors.
  • Computational Reinforcement Learning 2015Describes learning as an algorithm in which an agent tries things in an environment and maximizes cumulative reward, so that psychological learning theory and artificial intelligence share the same mathematics.
