Clinical and Mental Health

Thread

How is suffering understood and healed?

Schools (by date)

  • Birth of Clinical Psychology 1896Psychology no longer studies only 'ordinary people' in the laboratory. It begins to assess and help specific individuals who are struggling.
  • Modern Psychiatric Classification 1899Mental disorders are not independent symptoms. They can be grouped into different 'disease units' according to onset, course and outcome.
  • Morita Therapy 1919The more anxiety is pushed away, the worse it gets. The principle is 'go with the flow, do what needs to be done': accept that the symptoms exist while doing what needs doing.
  • DSM / ICD Diagnostic Systems 1952A diagnostic manual that specifies 'what counts as a particular mental disorder,' used for clinical work, research, insurance and communication. It is also criticized for 'putting labels on suffering.'
  • Cognitive Therapies 1955Emotions are not caused directly by events but by our interpretation of events. Therapy helps people identify and test these automatic thoughts.
  • Family Systems Therapy 1956A person's symptoms are often an expression of the family as a whole. Changing the interaction patterns is more effective than treating the identified patient alone.
  • Common Factors 1961Different therapies are all effective because they share: a good relationship, hope and expectation, a credible explanation, and opportunities for action.
  • Biopsychosocial Model 1977Illness and health cannot be explained by the body alone. They must be viewed at three levels, biological, psychological and social, and in terms of their interaction.
  • Cognitive-Behavioral Therapy 1977Integrates behavioral techniques (exposure, behavioral activation) with cognitive techniques (testing thoughts) in a structured, time-limited, goal-oriented format.
  • Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction 1979An eight-week group course that teaches people to deliberately and nonjudgmentally direct attention to the present breath, body and thoughts, in order to cope with stress and pain.
  • Trauma Studies and PTSD 1980Extreme threatening experiences can leave lasting symptoms: intrusive memories, avoidance, hypervigilance and emotional numbing. Recognizing this as a real disorder changed the situation of victims of war and violence.
  • Third-Wave Therapies 1991Rather than mainly changing the content of thoughts, these therapies change a person's relationship to thoughts and emotions: acceptance, mindfulness, defusion, and acting toward valued goals.
  • Evidence-Based Practice in Psychology 1995Clinical decisions should combine three things: the best available research evidence, the therapist's professional experience, and the patient's characteristics and preferences.
  • Dimensional Models (RDoC / HiTOP) 2010Mental problems are not categories of 'present or absent' but continuous dimensions (such as depression, fear and thought disorder). Research should classify by function and brain circuits rather than by diagnostic labels.
  • Unified Protocol 2011Different anxiety and depressive disorders share mechanisms such as emotional avoidance and rumination, so the same set of skills training is used to address many problems.
  • Digital Mental Health 2017Provides assessment and treatment through programs, phone apps or online courses, so that regions with few resources can also get support, while raising issues of data privacy and quality.
  • Process-Based Therapy 2018Stops arguing about which therapy is better and asks: for this person, which processes of change (such as flexibility and motivation) need to change? Then it selects the matching methods.
  • ICD-11 and Complex PTSD 2019The World Health Organization's new diagnostic system lists the consequences of long-term repeated trauma separately as 'complex PTSD,' and restructures many diagnoses.
  • Psychedelic-Assisted Therapy 2021Under the supervision of trained therapists, patients take drugs such as MDMA or psilocybin, combined with psychotherapy before and after, for trauma and depression.
