Carl Gustav Jung

Person·1875–1961·卡尔·古斯塔夫·荣格

Swiss psychiatrist who founded analytical psychology; early in his career he worked at the Burghölzli hospital

Lived
1875–1961
Schools
Analytical Psychology

Theories

  • Jung's Model of the Psyche 1912The psyche includes the conscious ego, the personal unconscious (which contains complexes), and the collective unconscious (which contains archetypes). The task of life is individuation.
  • Collective Unconscious and Archetypes 1919Humans share an innate psychological structure, which appears as recurring images such as the mother, the hero, the wise old man, and the shadow.
  • Psychological Types 1921People have two attitudes, introversion and extraversion, and four functions: thinking, feeling, sensation, and intuition. Each person leans toward one or two of them. The MBTI was built on this framework.
  • Word Association and Complexes 1906When certain words touch emotional issues, people respond more slowly or cannot answer. Jung called these clusters of emotionally charged memories 'complexes'. Around 1907, Jung also used a galvanometer to record changes in skin conductance when subjects heard stimulus words, measuring emotional reactions this way.
  • Synchronicity 1952Two events with no causal link occur together because of their meaning. For example, you dream of someone and that same day you receive a letter from them.
  • Stages of Life and the Midlife Transition 1930The first half of life is spent building a career and adapting to the outer world. After midlife, the task turns inward, toward finding life's meaning and moving toward individuation.
  • Active Imagination 1916While awake, the person lets inner images arise on their own, then engages with them through painting, writing, or dialogue. This brings unconscious content into awareness.
