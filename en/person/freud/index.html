Sigmund Freud

Person·1856–1939·西格蒙德·弗洛伊德

Austrian neurologist and founder of psychoanalysis

Lived
1856–1939
Schools
Classical Psychoanalysis

Theories

  • The Interpretation of Dreams 1900A dream is a disguised fulfillment of an unconscious wish. Interpreting a dream means tracing from its manifest content back to its latent content.
  • Drive Theory and Unconscious Conflict 1905People are driven by instincts for sex and aggression, which conflict with the demands of reality and morality. The conflict is pushed into the unconscious and then appears as symptoms, dreams, and slips of the tongue.
  • Structural Model: Id, Ego, Superego 1923The mind is divided into the id, which seeks gratification; the ego, which takes reality into account; and the superego, which internalizes norms. Symptoms arise from conflict among the three.
  • Transference 1912Patients bring feelings and expectations formed toward important people in early life into their relationship with the therapist. The therapist can use this to see the patient's patterns.
  • Repression 1915Painful material is pushed out of consciousness and stored in the unconscious. It may later return under suitable conditions.
  • Oedipus Complex 1910Children around three to four are attracted to the opposite-sex parent and hostile toward the same-sex parent. How these conflicts are resolved determines later personality.
