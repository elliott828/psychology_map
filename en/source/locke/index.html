Empiricism and Associationism

Source·1690·经验论与联想主义

Locke's An Essay Concerning Human Understanding argues that the mind begins as a blank slate, and that ideas come from experience and their associations. Hume, Mill and others developed these associations into associationism, the ancestor of behaviorist views of learning.

When
1690
What remains today
The idea that experience shapes the mind appears in behaviorism and connectionism: association entered learning theory, and neural networks learn by changing the strengths of connections. The 'blank slate' debate still recurs in discussions of nature and nurture.
Later influenced
Law of Effect
