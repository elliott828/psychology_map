Humoral Theory and Temperament

Source·around 400 BCE·体液说与气质

The Hippocratic school explained temperament and disease through four body fluids: blood, phlegm, yellow bile and black bile. The later 'four temperaments' theory is the source of typological thinking about temperament. Eysenck once compared his own dimensions with the four temperaments.

When
around 400 BCE
What remains today
Temperament names such as 'sanguine' and 'choleric' still appear in everyday speech and in some popular personality tests. Serious personality research has long since moved to measurable dimensions such as the Big Five, but the idea that people differ in stable temperament types comes from this tradition.
Later influenced
