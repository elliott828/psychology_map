Smartphones and Adolescent Mental Health

Theory·2017·智能手机与青少年心理健康

Argues that since the early 2010s, the spread of smartphones and social media has left teenagers lonelier, more anxious, and sleeping less. Critics argue the effect is very small and that other factors cannot be ruled out.

Historical influence ●●●○○·Evidence: Mixed findings·Public familiarity ●●●●○

School
Screens and Adolescent Mental Health
Landmark
Twenge and colleagues published analyses of rising depression and suicide-related outcomes in adolescents alongside smartphone adoption (2017–2018).
What the evidence says
The timing of the trends fits, but at the individual level the correlation between screen time and mental health is small. Researchers strongly disagree about the direction of causation and the size of the effect.
