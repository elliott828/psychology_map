Conditioned Emotional Response (Little Albert)

Theory·1920·条件性情绪反应（小阿尔伯特）

A baby was startled by a loud noise when he saw a white rat. After a few pairings, he cried whenever he saw a rat. This is cited as proof that phobias can be learned, but the evidence behind it is much thinner than textbooks suggest.

Historical influence ●●●○○·Evidence: Little research·Public familiarity ●●●●○

School
Classical Conditioning
Landmark
Watson and Rayner reported a baby who was taught to fear a white rat.
What the evidence says
There was only one subject, the procedure records are inconsistent, and the study was never successfully replicated. Its significance lies mainly in inspiring models of how fear is learned.
