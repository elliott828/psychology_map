Fight-or-Flight Response

Theory·1915·战或逃反应

When faced with a threat, the sympathetic nervous system and adrenaline speed up the heart and send blood to the muscles, preparing the body to fight or run.

Historical influence ●●●○○·Evidence: Strong evidence·Public familiarity ●●●●○

School
Stress Physiology
Landmark
Cannon, 'Bodily Changes in Pain, Hunger, Fear and Rage'.
What the evidence says
The evidence for the acute sympathetic and adrenaline response is solid. The idea that there are only two options, fight or flight, was later expanded to include freezing, appeasement, and others.

Key people
