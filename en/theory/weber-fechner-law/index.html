Weber-Fechner Law

Theory·1860·韦伯—费希纳定律

A stimulus must increase by a fixed proportion before a person notices a change. In a noisy room, you have to raise your voice a lot before anyone hears you.

Historical influence ●●●○○·Evidence: Supported, with limits·Public familiarity ●●○○○

School
Psychophysics
Landmark
Fechner extended Weber's findings into the claim that sensation is proportional to the logarithm of stimulus intensity.
What the evidence says
It holds roughly across the middle range of intensities. Stevens' later power function is more accurate in many cases.
