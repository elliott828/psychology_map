Charcot's Hysteria and Hypnosis

Theory·1887·沙可的癔症与催眠研究

Hysteria was not 'faking illness'. It was a real disorder that could be reproduced through hypnosis. Its symptoms came from psychological causes rather than bodily injury.

Historical influence ●●●○○·Evidence: Mostly historical·Public familiarity ●●○○○

School
Hypnosis, Hysteria and Dissociation
Landmark
Charcot's 'Tuesday lectures' at the Salpetriere Hospital.
What the evidence says
He treated hysteria as a real neurosis and brought it into modern medicine. But his demonstrations clearly involved suggestion, and the theory has been abandoned.
