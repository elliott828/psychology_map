Piaget's Four Stages

Theory·1947·认知发展四阶段

A child's thinking passes through four stages in order. First they learn about the world through action, then they use symbols, then they can carry out concrete logical operations, and finally they can reason abstractly.

Historical influence ●●●●○·Evidence: Supported, with limits·Public familiarity ●●●●○

School
Cognitive Development Theory
Landmark
Piaget, 'The Psychology of Intelligence', laid out the sensorimotor, preoperational, concrete operational, and formal operational stages.
What the evidence says
The idea that cognition undergoes qualitative change broadly holds. But the boundaries between stages are blurrier than he assumed, and children can accomplish some tasks at younger ages.
