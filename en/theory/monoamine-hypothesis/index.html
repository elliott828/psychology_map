Monoamine Hypothesis of Depression

Theory·1965·单胺（血清素）假说

Depression comes from a shortage of certain brain neurotransmitters, namely norepinephrine and serotonin. The idea inspired antidepressant drugs, but it is often misreported as a simple 'lack of happy chemicals'.

Historical influence ●●●○○·Evidence: Mixed findings·Public familiarity ●●●●●

School
Psychopharmacology
Landmark
Schildkraut published 'The Catecholamine Hypothesis of Affective Disorders'.
What the evidence says
Drugs do act on the monoamine system. But direct evidence that depression is caused by low serotonin is weak, and a 2022 review found no support for it.
